# Sprint 5.6.1 — Hand Tracking Interaction Refinement

**Status**: Completed

## Objective
Refine the overall hand-tracking experience by improving stability, responsiveness, and usability without changing the established architecture. The goal is to make hand-controlled gameplay feel significantly more natural and forgiving.

## Scope
- Centralized all magic numbers and interaction constants into `InteractionConfig`.
- Implemented temporary tracking persistence (180ms delay before dropping hand state) to eliminate pointer flickering caused by single-frame detection failures.
- Replaced the single pinch threshold with a Pinch Hysteresis system to eliminate rapid pinch/unpinch oscillation.
- Implemented Grab Persistence, maintaining a drag for 200ms when tracking is lost, preventing accidental drops.
- Implemented a larger, forgiving hover detection margin (48px) for easier targeting without affecting collision validation.
- Increased magnetic hover assistance to a stronger 75% easing.
- Dynamically scale the `PointerOverlay` cursor (Idle: 24px, Hover: 34px, Grab: 40px) to provide enhanced spatial feedback.
- Added a visual Drag Offset (+30px Y axis) so the user's hand/finger does not obscure the grabbed piece, mimicking modern VR/AR patterns.
- Refined animation curves and timing (e.g. `duration-300`, `easeOut`) for a smoother, production-quality presentation.

## Architecture Decisions
- No architecture rules were changed. The pipeline `MediaPipe -> HandTrackingProvider -> GestureRecognizer -> Pointer Events -> Puzzle Engine` remains fully intact.
- The drag offset was carefully implemented in the presentation layer (`PuzzlePiece.tsx`) without altering the canonical drag coordinates generated by the Gesture Recognizer, ensuring slot targeting behaves identically for mouse and hand interactions.
